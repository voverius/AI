
# Communication development references
For authors refining nemo-general. These guides inform development and evaluation only. They are
not runtime dependencies, and the skill must not require opening them to answer a user.

## Guides
- [GOV.UK: Use clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/) - familiar words, explicit requirements and direct language, including for specialists
- [Google: Short sentences](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/short-sentences) - one main idea per sentence, direct verbs and removal of words that add no meaning
- [NN/g: Concise, scannable and objective writing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/) - original web-usability research supporting useful structure and factual wording alongside brevity

Apply their useful principles rather than importing every house rule. Their web and documentation
contexts are not proof of improved chat comprehension. Do not turn historical usability percentages
or suggested sentence lengths into guarantees or compulsory targets.

## Design choices
Optimize for correct understanding with the least reading effort. A shorter reply loses if the
reader misses a condition, confuses what passed with what was not tested, or cannot identify the
next action. Keep the answer first, group related facts and choose concrete words.

The user's local preference allows useful emojis and symbols, such as a green check rather than
an ASCII imitation. This is a presentation decision, not a finding attributed to the guides.
Pair status symbols with clear text. A passed check does not establish an untested broader result.

Keep established scope and output choices: direct factual answers, concise progress, structured
review questions, and the requested number of complete alternatives. Do not replace these with one
universal template. Keep required caveats beside the claim they limit.

## Evaluation
Use realistic inputs with decisive facts specified before writing. Include a mixed pass/fail or
not-run status, a progress rewrite, a decision question, and a plain answer that needs no symbol.
Test whether compression preserves identities, numbers, units, causes, conditions and uncertainty.

Have a separate reader recover the important facts and intended action from the output before
seeing the input. Compare their answers with the input, then assess scanning, natural wording and
unnecessary text. Count words only after information coverage and correctness pass. Do not reward
icons, shorter text or checker acceptance by themselves.

When claiming improvement, compare equivalent inputs and conditions. Treat a mechanical reply
checker as a layout check, not proof of truth, clarity or zero confusion. Show representative
outputs to the user because their comprehension and preferences remain part of acceptance.
